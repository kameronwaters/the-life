# Did the John 21 fish fry really happen? — fact-check of the Creation Care Church talk

**Source checked:** Creation Care Church, Friday Night Live, "Did Jesus' fish fry in John 21 really happen?"
Speaker: Craig (Creation Care Church / Bible Origin channel). Talk given on a third-Friday live stream in
September 2026 (he closes with "see you the first Friday of October"; annual meeting Oct 9). Cleaned
transcript: [sources/creation-care-church-john-21-talk-transcript.md](./sources/creation-care-church-john-21-talk-transcript.md)

**Checked:** 2026-10-04. Several primary-text sites were blocked from this machine (newadvent, ccel,
tertullian.org, biblehub, wikipedia). Where I could not read a source directly I say so below and
cite the secondary source I used instead.

---

## Bottom line

The talk's **scripture quotations and patristic quotations are accurate**. Its **core scholarly claim is
mainstream**: most Johannine scholars treat John 21 as an epilogue added after the gospel's original
ending at 20:30-31. But the talk **overstates the patristic silence, gets two factual points wrong
(Sea of Tiberias, Peter in Mark), stretches the geography, and leans on a fringe dating** (end of
the 2nd century, Luke written c. 170 from Marcion) that the manuscript and citation evidence does not
allow. Use the strong parts; drop the weak ones.

What survives as a usable argument:

1. John 20:30-31 reads as a formal ending; John 21 is a second ending. Majority scholarly view.
2. John 21 imports Mark/Luke material (fishermen disciples, sons of Zebedee, miraculous catch) that
   the first 20 chapters of John never use. Textually true.
3. John 21 does visible repair work: Peter's three-fold restoration answers the three-fold denial;
   21:23 corrects a rumor that a disciple would live until the return; 21:24 attaches authorship.
4. Tertullian (c. 213) calls John 20:31 the "conclusion" (*clausula*) of the gospel.
5. The Greek of the meal is `opsarion` / `prosphagion` (relish eaten with bread), not `ichthys`,
   in 21:5, 9, 10, 13. See the Christspiracy KB note on this.

What does not survive:

- "Nobody in the 2nd century knew John 21." Clement of Alexandria quotes John 21:4-5 (*Paedagogus* 1.5,
  c. 190s), and Papyrus 66 (c. 200) contains John 21:1-9, 12, 17.
- "The chapter was added at the very end of the 2nd century." Scholars who call it an addition date it
  c. 100-125, inside the Johannine community, and every manuscript ever found contains it.
- "Sea of Tiberias was not a name until the 2nd century." Josephus uses it in the 70s CE and John 6:1 uses it.
- "Peter is never mentioned again in Mark after the denial." Mark 16:7 names him.

### B. Patristic and manuscript claims

| # | Claim in talk | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Tertullian, *Against Praxeas* 25: "this Gospel, at its very termination, intimate[s] ... 'that ye might believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God'" | CONFIRMED | ANF wording matches. Latin: *ipsa quoque clausula evangelii ... ut credatis Iesum Christum filium dei*. Work dated c. 213. Souter's edition footnotes: "It is unsafe to conclude from this expression that Tertullian was unacquainted with the twenty-first chapter." *Clausula* can mean the gospel's closing statement of purpose, not its last line. |
| B2 | Tertullian is also the earliest witness to John 21 | CONFIRMED that he cites it; NOT CONFIRMED that he is earliest | *Scorpiace* 15 (c. 203-208): "Then is Peter girt by another, when he is made fast to the cross" (John 21:18). *Prescription Against Heretics* 22 (c. 200) alludes to 21:20 or 13:23. But Clement of Alexandria, *Paedagogus* 1.5 (c. 190s) quotes John 21:4-5 ("Children, have ye any meat?"). Clement is probably earlier than Tertullian. |
| B3 | "Nobody in the 2nd century mentions John 21" | NOT CONFIRMED | Clement of Alexandria wrote *Paedagogus* before 200. Papyrus 66 (dated c. 200; Hunger argued mid-2nd century) contains 20:25-21:9, 12, 17. Papyrus 109 (3rd c.) has 21:18-20, 23-25. |
| B4 | Justin Martyr cites "18 of the first 20 chapters" of John | NOT CONFIRMED | No scholarly tally supports this. Whether Justin used John at all is debated: Koester and Bellinzoni say no; Hill and Kruger say yes, chiefly from 1 Apol. 61 (John 3:3-5) and Dial. 88, 105. His silence on ch. 21 proves nothing either way. |
| B5 | Irenaeus (180s) cites "all 20 chapters, multiple times" but never ch. 21 | SILENCE PLAUSIBLE; TALLY UNVERIFIED; ONE CAVEAT | **Silence:** I found no clear quotation of John 21 in Irenaeus. The e-Catena index lists none, and no scholar I found cites one. Irenaeus does quote John 20:31 verbatim (*Against Heresies* 3.16.5), so he knew the ch. 20 ending. **Caveat:** *AH* 3.1.1 calls John the disciple "who also leaned upon his breast" (Greek via Eusebius *HE* 5.8.4: *ho kai epi to stethos autou anapeson*). John 13:25 has *epi to stethos tou Iesou*; John 21:20 has *hos kai anepesen ... epi to stethos autou*. Irenaeus's "also" (*kai*) and "his" (*autou*) match 21:20, not 13:25. Bauckham reads it as 13:25; the wording argues for 21:20. If 21:20 is right, Irenaeus knew ch. 21 and the silence collapses. **Tally:** "every chapter, multiple times" I could not verify. Spot checks found Irenaeus quoting chs. 1, 2, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 14, 15, 16, 17, 19 (19:34), 20. Mutschler's 2004 register counts 309 Johannine references; I could not read it. Argument from silence either way. |
| B6 | Helmut Koester: John 21 "though present in all extant manuscripts is also widely recognized as a later addition"; 20:30-31 original ending | CONFIRMED in substance | Koester, *Introduction to the New Testament* vol. 2 (cited for this on the John 21 Wikipedia page). Could not read the page to check exact wording. |
| B7 | Scholars "pretty much unanimously" agree ch. 21 is later | CONFIRMED, overstated | Majority, not unanimous. Bauckham ("The 153 Fish and the Unity of the Fourth Gospel"), Carson, Keener, Stibbe defend unity. Even many who call it an epilogue (Brown) keep it inside the Johannine school, c. 100-110, not 180-200. |
| B8 | No manuscript lacks John 21 | CONFIRMED, with one live debate | True. But Brent Nongbri (*Early Christianity* 9.3, 2018) argues that P66 has an unusually large blank space after 20:31 and starts 21:1 on a new page, which could mean its exemplar ended at 20:31. Larry Hurtado replied that scribes mark major sense-units with space, so it may show only that ch. 21 was read as a new unit. Another response (Academia: "Does P66 Suggest a Vorlage Lacking John 21?") disputes Nongbri's page reconstruction. So: a hint, not a 20-chapter manuscript. Still, a chapter inserted c. 190 could not have reached every manuscript line, including P66 in Egypt c. 200, with no surviving 20-chapter copy. |
| B9 | Acts of Peter 37-38: "crucify me head downward" | CONFIRMED | Acts of Peter / Martyrdom of Peter, c. 180-190. The John 21:18 prediction does not say "upside down"; the talk is right that the detail comes from the apocryphal Acts. |
| B10 | Clementine Homilies: Peter eats only bread, olives, rarely pot-herbs | CONFIRMED text; dating problem | Hom. 12.6: "I use only bread and olives, and rarely pot-herbs." Recog. 7.6 parallel. But the Homilies are a 4th-century redaction of a 3rd-century base (Grundschrift c. 220-260). Not "Peter's own account". A real 2nd-century source for a vegetarian leader is Hegesippus on James (in Eusebius, *Hist. Eccl.* 2.23): "drank no wine ... ate no flesh". |
| B11 | Marcion's gospel had the miraculous catch; Luke copied it c. 170 | CONFIRMED that Marcion's gospel had the scene; FRINGE on dating | Tertullian, *Against Marcion* 4.9, says Marcion's text had Peter trembling "at the very large draught of fishes" and "fear not, henceforth thou shalt catch men" (Luke 5:9-10). Mainstream: Marcion abridged Luke (Luke c. 80-90). Marcion priority is a minority view (Klinghardt, Vinzent, BeDuhn in part). |
| B12 | Robert Eisenman: "sons of Zebedee" an invented doublet | CONFIRMED attribution | Eisenman (*James the Brother of Jesus*) argues James son of Zebedee is a literary double created to displace James the brother of Jesus. His "Zebedee-dooda" tag I could not verify. |

### C. Historical / geographic framing

| # | Claim in talk | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | "Sea of Tiberias" name "didn't exist until the 2nd century" | FALSE | Tiberias founded c. 17-20 CE (Josephus, *Ant.* 18.36). Josephus, *War* 3.57 and 4.456 (written c. 75-79 CE): "lake of Tiberias". John 6:1 itself: "Sea of Galilee (that is, the Sea of Tiberias)"; John 6:23: "boats from Tiberias". Pausanias 5.7.4 (2nd c.) also uses it. Using this to date ch. 21 backfires, since ch. 6 has the same name. |
| C2 | Bethany beyond the Jordan to Galilee is "about 125 miles, a week's walk" | CONFIRMED, overstated | Sea of Galilee to Dead Sea is 65-70 miles (105-117 km) straight line; Al-Maghtas sits 5-6 miles north of the Dead Sea. So roughly 60-65 miles direct, perhaps 80-90 by valley road: 3-4 days, not a week. Rainer Riesner (Tyndale Bulletin 38, 1987) argues "Bethany beyond the Jordan" is Batanea, north-east of Galilee, which would make the trip short. The location is disputed. |
| C3 | In John the disciples "are not Galilean fishermen" | HALF RIGHT | John 1-20 never calls them fishermen and never names Zebedee. But John 1:44 makes Bethsaida (Galilee) "the city of Andrew and Peter", and John 2:1-12, 4:43-54 keep them in Galilee. Not fishermen (in John), but Galileans. |
| C4 | John 21:23 requires all the disciples to be dead, so mid-2nd century | NOT CONFIRMED | The rumor concerns one man, the beloved disciple. His death (tradition: Ephesus, c. 98-100) is enough to explain 21:23. That fits the usual c. 100-110 dating, not 150-200. |
| C5 | Reason for the addition: Rome asserting Peter over James | PLAUSIBLE, not provable | Peter's restoration and the beloved disciple's status are both real concerns of ch. 21. Rome vs Jerusalem is one reading; Johannine-community vs Petrine-church tension (Brown) is the mainstream one. |
| C6 | Luke written c. 170 to Theophilus of Antioch (bishop 168/169-182/183) | NOT CONFIRMED | The bishop's dates are right. The identification is speculation with no ancient support. |
| C7 | "All the earliest sources" describe the disciples as vegan Nazarenes | NOT CONFIRMED | Earliest vegetarian testimonies: Hegesippus on James (2nd c., via Eusebius); Epiphanius on Ebionites (4th c.); Pseudo-Clementines (3rd-4th c.). Nothing from Paul, Mark, Q, or the first-century letters says so. "All the earliest sources" is too strong. |

## How I would use this

**Safe to say:**
- Most scholars think John ended at 20:31 and ch. 21 is an epilogue added by the Johannine community a generation later.
- The epilogue imports Synoptic fishing material that the first twenty chapters never use.
- Tertullian, writing around 213, calls 20:31 the gospel's conclusion.
- The Greek word for what is on the fire is the word for a bread-relish, and the scene says Jesus distributed it; it never says Jesus ate.

**Do not say:**
- That no one before 200 knew ch. 21 (Clement, P66).
- That Irenaeus certainly did not know it. His silence is likely but *AH* 3.1.1 may echo 21:20; say "no clear citation", not "no knowledge".
- That the chapter was added at the end of the 2nd century (manuscripts and citations make it earlier).
- That "Sea of Tiberias" dates anything (Josephus, John 6:1).
- That Mark drops Peter after the denial (Mark 16:7).

**Still to check when sources are reachable:**
- Koester's exact sentence in *Introduction to the NT* vol. 2.
- Irenaeus chapter-by-chapter tally (Mutschler, *Irenäus als johanneischer Theologe*, 2004; Biblia Patristica vol. 1) and whether anyone besides me reads *AH* 3.1.1 as 21:20.
- Nongbri's P66 article and the reply disputing his page reconstruction.
- Clement, *Paedagogus* 1.5 wording in Greek.
